# Harvest 03 — what the previous round had, and where the concept stands on it

A **comparison**, not a harvest. Nothing here has been written into the concept, and nothing
here should be without an owner decision and a new `D-<nnn>` ([PR-1](../../../CLAUDE.md),
[PR-3](../../../CLAUDE.md)). Legacy is quoted so the row is recognisable; it is never inherited.

**The owner's framing, which governs the verdicts.** Much of the legacy documents *a problem
being worked around* rather than a considered design — the technique was not right yet, and the
round went in spirals. So a row counts as **missing** only when a **user need** can be named:
something a person wanted to do. A need of the old implementation, or of the old planning
process, is a **workaround** instead, and those are evidence of what the new design must simply
not need.

Every **missing** row below was checked against the decision log first; the entries searched are
named in its Detail cell, so a false gap costs the owner nothing.

**The shape of that.** Two rows in three are *covered* or *deliberately dropped* — the concept
has been over most of this ground already, usually from the owner's own statements rather than
from the old files. A further twelve are **workarounds**, which is the round's own verdict on
itself: they exist because everything had to be a WordPress term. Eleven genuine gaps out of
seven and a half thousand lines is the useful yield.

Plus **12 topics that never held** — re-decided two to four times in the legacy — in a separate
table at the end. Those are one finding each, not one per answer.
